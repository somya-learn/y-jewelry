Y · CREATIVE DIRECTION · IMAGERY, WEBSITE AND CASTING

The Unsaid.

Y photographs the moment just before something is said, or just after. A wrist turned toward the light. A cup going cold. A clasp being closed by someone else’s hands. We show everything except the reason.

The idea in one line: other brands show you what a piece means. Y shows you that it means something, and keeps the rest private. Where pictures come from: you may choose a stock image, make one with AI, or have them photographed, as long as every picture looks real. “I make the charms. I don’t tell anyone what they mean. Our pictures should do the same.” Monika

IMAGERY ACROSS THE WEBSITE

Three kinds of picture, one light

Every image on the site belongs to one of three registers. Pages are paced by moving between them: never two images of the same register side by side, and every page holds at least one moment of stillness.

REGISTER I Close The piece and the skin, and nothing else.
  • Macro and near-macro on wrist, collarbone and palm.
  • One light, from the left, the way she reads. Shadow allowed to fall.
  • Every finish true: silver white, gold warm yellow, rose gold pink-copper.
  • Same ground, light and scale for every charm, so the collection reads as one hand.
Lives on: product pages, charm grids, set pages (The Story, The Chapter, The First Page), Your Story, search
REGISTER II Kept Objects that hold time.
  • Still life on wood, limewash and cotton: the Y box, the recyclable envelope, handwriting, steel and brass.
  • Quiet, top-down or low three-quarter; generous empty space. Flat lays are welcome, on Instagram too: linen or limewash, a few real everyday objects, never clutter.
  • Only what really exists and ships. Nothing staged that she won’t find in her own home or her own box.
Lives on: packaging, gift, checkout, Care guide, Hand-finished, Recycled silver, BIS hallmarked
REGISTER III Lived Her own light, between moments.
  • Women in real Indian homes and streets: a window, a stairwell, a kitchen at 7 am, a scooter at a signal.
  • Turned away, mid-gesture, slightly out of focus. Never posing, never looking into the lens.
  • Y in the frame, never the subject. Sometimes it isn’t visible at all.
Lives on: homepage, Stories between us, In their words, the Story Circle, Monika’s notes, Instagram
The homepage, in order
  • Lived: the newest set, worn, in morning light (full width).
  • Close: The Story, The Chapter and The First Page in the same pose, side by side.
  • Kept: the box, opened.
  • Lived: a woman turned away, beside Monika’s latest note.
A product page, in order
  • Close: the charm on limewash, true finish.
  • Close: in the palm, for scale.
  • Close: on the wrist, in jaali light.
  • Close: on the necklace, at the collarbone.
Motion
  • Three-second loops, never longer: a charm swaying, a dupatta passing, light moving across a jaali.
  • No music, no voice, no text over the film.
  • The two packaging films follow the Kept register.

CASTING · MODELS MOODBOARD AND BRIEF

Cast women, not models

We cast for hands, wrists and the way someone holds a cup, not for faces. The ideal Y model is a woman you’d stand behind in a queue and wonder about. Street casting and real friends and families first; agencies second.

Fair to wheatishWarm undertone.
Wheatish to duskyMost of India. Most of our casting.
Dusky to deepLit warmly so silver glows.
30 to 55
Her ageWomen in their thirties, forties and early fifties: building, raising, beginning again.
Two, together
PairsMother and daughter, sisters, old friends. Real ones, if they’ll agree.
Hands that have done things
Hand modelsNatural, kept, not perfect. A visible vein or knuckle is welcome.
Face and hair
  • Bare or near-bare skin; texture and the odd mark left in.
  • Hair as she’d wear it on a Sunday: loose, a low bun, an oiled plait.
  • Short, clean nails with clear nail paint only. No colour, no extensions, no nail art.
Jewellery on set
  • Only Y on the wrist and neck. No ring.
  • No bangles, watches, other bracelets or heavy gold.
  • Every piece styled exactly as a woman would wear it, never stacked for the camera.
Direction on set
  • Give her a task, not a pose: pour the chai, find the page, close the clasp.
  • Shoot through the moment; choose the frame after it, not the peak.
  • Faces withheld on Close images: crop at the lips or turn her away. Full faces only in Lived.
Stock and AI
  • Both are allowed, as long as they look real: true skin and hands, believable light, real Indian rooms and streets.
  • Any Y piece in an image must look exactly like the real piece: same shape, size and finish.
  • If it looks generated or staged, it doesn’t go on the site.
CLOTHES BRIEF

Clothes she already owns

The clothes are never the story. They should look like the best things in her own wardrobe: natural fabric, a little lived-in, chosen so the wrist and the collarbone are free and the silver leads.

IvoryThe base of most looks.
OatSoft, warm neutral.
SandLinen and khadi.
ClayWarmth near the skin.
Muted oliveA quiet earthy contrast.
IndigoThe one deep colour.
Dusty blushOnly beside rose gold.
What she wears
  • A straight or short kurta in mulmul or handloom cotton.
  • A washed white or ivory shirt, sleeves rolled.
  • Linen trousers or a relaxed cotton dress.
  • A plain handloom saree with a narrow border, for a Lived or Story Circle frame.
  • A soft dupatta or cotton stole as a layer she can move with.
Fabric and fit
  • Cotton, mulmul, khadi, linen, chanderi, soft silk-cotton.
  • Relaxed, never tight; real creases are fine, steamed not starched.
  • Matte surfaces only, so nothing competes with the shine of silver.
For each piece
  • Bracelets: sleeves three-quarter, rolled or sleeveless. Nothing at the wrist.
  • Necklaces: round, scoop, V or open-collar necklines; nothing high or buttoned to the neck.
  • Both: no frills, ties or embellishment where the piece sits.
Never
  • Prints, logos, slogans or visible brand labels.
  • Sequins, zari, mirror work, heavy embroidery or shiny synthetics.
  • Sheer or very revealing clothes.
  • Black, neon or saturated colours.
  • Festive or bridal wear.

WHAT Y IS NOT

The images we never make

The festive clichéNo diyas, marigolds, lehengas and gold overload. Y is worn on ordinary days.
The luxury clichéNo marble, velvet boxes, champagne, rose petals or black backdrops.
The meaning propNo zodiac wheels, four-leaf clovers, hearts drawn in sand or quote cards beside a charm.
The model momentNo pout, no stare into the lens, no laughing at nothing. She is never performing.
The fakeNo plastic skin, extra fingers, impossible light, or a piece Y doesn’t sell: no gemstones, earrings or handbags.
REFERENCES TO SHARE WITH THE PHOTOGRAPHER
  • The intimacy and lived-in interiors of Dayanita Singh’s photographs.
  • Window light and stillness in Vermeer’s women at work.
  • The quiet domestic rooms of Satyajit Ray’s films.
  • “Ma”: the Japanese idea of meaningful empty space.
For mood only. We never copy a frame.
THE TEST FOR EVERY IMAGE

Does it make you want to know her story, and feel it would be wrong to ask?

If yes, it’s Y. If it tells you the story, it isn’t.
